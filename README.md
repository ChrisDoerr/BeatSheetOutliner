## A Note on Story Structure

This tool uses Blake Snyder’s *Save the Cat!* beat sheet as a foundation for outlining stories. It is intended as a **storytelling aid, not a set of hard rules**.

The beats — and especially their commonly suggested positions or percentage ranges — should be treated as guidelines, not laws. Stories are messy, characters are unpredictable, and good storytelling does not always fit neatly into a predefined structure.

Most importantly, the beats should **emerge naturally from the story**, rather than the story being bent to satisfy the beats.

In other words: just because a particular beat is “supposed” to happen at a certain point does not mean that the plot, characters, or pacing should be forced into making it happen there. If the characters and their journey naturally arrive at a beat earlier, later, or somewhere in between, the story should take precedence over the outline.

Think of the beat sheet as a **map, not a railroad track**. It can help you see where you are, what might be missing, and where the story could go next — but it should never dictate the journey.

This tool is simply an outlining aid for writers who find this kind of structure useful. If you work well with beats and story frameworks, hopefully it helps you think through your story. If you don't, that's perfectly fine too.
